# Research memo

Lead with the answer supported by the material actually read. Scale the format to the request; do not force a long memo for a simple question.

For a substantive memo, organize the evidence around issues:

| Issue | Retrieved authority and exact pinpoint | Supported conclusion | Material gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User's issue | Canonical instrument, Pasal/Ayat and supplied reader link | Text-based conclusion, with interpretation labelled | Missing text, uncertain status, incomplete relationships or unresolved jurisdiction |

Follow with short operative Indonesian quotations, relevant recorded status/amendment evidence, and the remaining research scope. An English answer can translate a clause, but preserve its Indonesian quotation and identify the translation.

The tool's `source.official_url` or `source.pdf_url`, when present and not withheld, can support source verification; do not manufacture those fields or URLs. Prefer supplied Pasal.id reader links for user navigation. A missing provenance field means unavailable provenance, not a license to infer it.

For local instruments, ensure the selected issuing locality matches the request. National law can be contextual evidence; distinguish it from the requested local rule. For several ministries or common instrument numbers, compare the returned issuing body and identity rather than accepting the first hit.

Record only meaningful limits: what was searched/read, which requested text was missing, and how uncertainty affects the answer. Avoid claiming exhaustive coverage, current legal force, or an exact historical consolidation from a bounded search.

For MK or MA requests requiring judgment reasoning, explain the decision-reader limitation. The MK discovery workflow can identify cases, but an outcome category is not the holding.
